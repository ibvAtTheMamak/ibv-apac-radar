Topic 1 · From Malaysia (Oct 26, 26, 27) and Singapore (Oct 27)

Malaysia's renewables demand is being pulled from two directions: data centres at home and Singapore next door

Four entries this week show the same demand picture from different angles, with the grid and bankability as the common constraint. On the data centre side, TNB said on 27 October that it commits to connecting ministry-approved data centres to the grid within one year, naming demand forecasting, network capacity planning and targeted infrastructure as the measures, and saying distribution networks need modernising so renewable interconnections and data centre load can be planned together. That follows last week's entry showing 12.3 GW of data centre supply capacity already approved, with a requirement that data centres source at least 30% renewable energy. On the Singapore side, Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof said on 26 October that Malaysia will help facilitate a 300 MW floating solar project at the Linggiu Dam in Johor, and that both countries will proceed with a second subsea interconnector now that its study is complete; the Energy Commission and EMA also exchanged MoUs. The same day, Maybank, DBS and IFC signed a memorandum with Ditrolic Energy on a financing framework for the US$6 billion Southern Johor Renewable Energy Corridor, which supplies Malaysia and Singapore. Its working group will cover regulatory approvals, grid interconnection, offtake, risk allocation and financing structure, and Ditrolic says close to 90% of the project's green energy capacity has attracted booking interest. Singapore's updated REC standard, SS 673, adds a demand-side rule: renewable electricity claims in Singapore should use RECs linked to power supplied to the Singapore grid, which includes approved low-carbon electricity import projects, while RECs from other overseas projects should not support those claims. Read together, data centres at home now have a one-year grid connection commitment, Johor-based export projects sit in the category Singapore's standard recognises, and in both cases the entries keep coming back to grid build-out and bankability.

Topic 2 · From the Philippines MGEN entry (Oct 29)

Meralco's MGEN is moving five solar companies into one listed vehicle, with a re-IPO as early as Q1 2027

MGEN Renewables' board approved on 27 October a P4.98 billion share swap in which MGreen, the Meralco Group's renewable energy arm, receives 3.11 billion new MGENR shares at P1.60 each, a 30.08% premium to the 23 October close, in exchange for its holdings in five solar companies. MGEN's CEO said MGENR could re-IPO as early as the first quarter of 2027 and would be the surviving entity of the planned consolidation, positioning it as the primary listed renewable platform of Meralco PowerGen. The assets being injected are operating, cash-generating solar companies, which is the stated aim of the swap. Separately, Terra Solar Philippines, majority-owned by MGENR and developing the P200 billion MTerra Solar project, is set to secure up to P7.82 billion in additional funding. The sequence follows MTerra Solar Phase 1 declaring commercial operations on 3 September, with 600 MWac delivered to Meralco under a PSA, and the 30 September MoU with VITRO REIT to study renewable-powered data centres.

Topic 3 · From Indonesia (Oct 26, Oct 19)

Indonesia's stance on Singapore exports shifted within a week: from waiting on price to doubting exports will happen soon

On 19 October, Energy Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said negotiations on electricity exports to Singapore had not reached a deal because the price was not yet clear, and that Indonesia would send the electricity once it was. Seven Indonesian companies held Singapore conditional approvals covering about 3.4 GW, with initial deliveries expected at around 600 MW. On 26 October he told reporters that Indonesia is still evaluating the stages of exports, that domestic demand is large, and that he doubts exports will happen in the near future, with everything to be reassessed; he did not name Singapore. Indonesia had planned to supply at least 3.4 GW of green power to Singapore by 2035, against Singapore's plan to import 6 GW of low-carbon electricity by 2035 with Malaysia, Vietnam and Australia among other likely suppliers. Bahlil had earlier said the tariff should be set by Jakarta as supplier. The article places the statement amid Indonesian haze and natural gas export cuts to Singapore and calls it a setback for ASEAN grid connection goals. It also cuts across earlier Singapore-facing moves in the radar: Tuas Power's PPA for Batam-area solar with imports from 2027 (16 September) and Keppel's structuring of an Indonesian solar supply chain for Jurong Island (18 September). Over the same days, the routes through Malaysia advanced: the Linggiu and Johor corridor items in Topic 1, and the Sembcorp approval for the Vietnam-Malaysia-Singapore project on 29 October.

Econergy Plans 150 MW Wind Farm in Northern Samar: P5.67B ($99M), 30 x 5 MW Turbines Across Allen, Victoria, San Isidro and Lavezares, Per DENR Filings

Econergy Renewable Power Philippines Inc. is planning a 150 MW wind farm in Northern Samar province with an estimated investment of P5.67 billion ($99 million), according to filings with the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR). The project will cover 3,159 hectares across the municipalities of Allen, Victoria, San Isidro and Lavezares, with 30 wind turbines of 5 MW each and two substations. Econergy said the project is intended to address power supply constraints at local and regional scale, and that it selected the site based on wind potential while seeking to minimise displacement of nearby communities. Construction is expected to begin after completion of detailed engineering designs, site plans and contractor procurement; no start date was specified.

TNB Pledges to Connect Ministry-Approved Data Centres to the Grid Within One Year; Says Strong Domestic Networks Key to ASEAN Power Grid Benefits

TNB chief distribution network officer Ahmad Sazree Abd Aziz said at a Singapore International Energy Week 2026 panel on 27 October that TNB commits to connecting data centres to the grid within one year once they are approved by the ministry-chaired committee, citing data centre demand pressure in Malaysia. He said the real benefit of the ASEAN Power Grid depends on strong domestic transmission and distribution networks. He named three measures for preparing the network for demand growth: demand forecasting, network capacity planning and targeted infrastructure development. TNB also needs to modernise and digitalise its distribution networks to facilitate RE interconnections at distribution level, and to align transmission and distribution planning so the system can absorb data centre demand and renewable energy integration.

Singapore EMA Grants Sembcorp Utilities Conditional Approval as Importer for Vietnam-Malaysia-Singapore Project: Up to 1.3 GW, Vietnamese Offshore Wind Plus Malaysian Solar and BESS, Imports from 2035

Singapore's Energy Market Authority (EMA) granted Sembcorp Utilities Pte Ltd (SCU) conditional approval to act as electricity importer for the Vietnam-Malaysia-Singapore (VMS) renewable energy export project, announced during Singapore International Energy Week 2026 (26 to 30 October). The VMS consortium comprises PetroVietnam Technical Services Corporation (PTSC, a Petrovietnam subsidiary) and SCU; it builds on a Joint Development Agreement signed in May 2025 with the MY Energy Consortium (TNB and PETRONAS). The project aims to deliver up to 1.3 GW of renewable electricity to Singapore through a transmission corridor linking Vietnam, Malaysia and Singapore. Most of the power would come from offshore wind in Vietnam, supplemented by solar and BESS in Malaysia; power generated in Vietnam would pass through Malaysia, which would consume part of the supply and act as the transit country for exports to Singapore. Imports are expected to begin in 2035.

KOMIPO Holds Seminar on VPP-Based Future Power Businesses with Industry and Academia; Plans to Build VPP into a Base for New Power Businesses

Korea Midland Power (KOMIPO) held a seminar on virtual power plant (VPP) based future power businesses on 27 October at its Seoul Power Station, discussing electricity market rule changes and ways to commercialise VPP. Participants agreed that, with distributed generation growing quickly, generation forecasting, demand adjustment and ESS use have become more important, and that operating individual generation assets separately makes grid stability hard to maintain; discussion covered concrete ways to turn VPP into a business model. KOMIPO plans to develop VPP beyond a management platform into a base for grid stabilisation and new power businesses, strengthening capability from resource acquisition through integrated operation, market participation and revenue model development, and working on technical and institutional constraints. KOMIPO president Lee Young-jo said the company will keep upgrading its VPP capability and strengthen cooperation with private sector and related bodies.

Singapore Updates SS 673 Code of Practice for RECs: Renewable Electricity Claims in Singapore Must Use RECs Linked to Power Supplied to the Singapore Grid

Enterprise Singapore, through the Singapore Standards Council, with METI and EMA, updated Singapore Standard SS 673: Code of Practice for Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs). The updated standard specifies which RECs can support claims of renewable electricity consumption in Singapore: companies should use RECs linked to renewable electricity supplied to Singapore's power grid. These include RECs from renewable electricity generated in Singapore, such as solar, and RECs from approved low-carbon electricity import projects. RECs from other overseas renewable energy projects that do not supply electricity into Singapore may still be purchased to support renewable energy development and regional decarbonisation, but should not be used to support claims of renewable electricity usage in Singapore. SS 673 was first launched in 2021. The amendments were developed by a public-private working group and went through public consultation.
