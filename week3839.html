Key Topics

Deeper cuts across this fortnight's entries, cross-checked against ib vogt's internal SharePoint datasets where available.

Topic 1 · From one entry each in Singapore and Indonesia (×2)

Singapore is increasingly running its own decarbonisation through Indonesian solar

Three entries this fortnight all point the same way: Tuas Power signed a PPA to buy renewable electricity from a $12.3B solar cluster near Batam, Riau Islands, with imports starting 2027 (Sep 16). Keppel is separately structuring an Indonesian solar project to feed its own Jurong Island CCGT plant and, from there, Singapore data-centre customers (Sep 18). And Singapore's EMA opened public consultation on a formal framework for cross-border ASEAN REC trading — the mechanism that would let Singapore-based buyers retire certificates from generators like these (Sep 18). None of these are new in concept — Vena Energy and ERA have been building Riau Islands export capacity for years — but three separate entities (a retailer, an integrated energy major, and the regulator itself) all advancing the same import dependency in the same fortnight suggests it's moving from pilot to standing policy.

Topic 2 · From four South Korea entries and one Malaysia entry

South Korea restructured its entire renewable-power support system in ten days

Four separate moves, one country, one fortnight: the 15-year-old Renewable Portfolio Standard — which cost KEPCO's five gencos a record KRW 2.06T in REC purchases in 2025 alone — will be scrapped for competitive auctions from 2027 (Sep 16). Those same five gencos (KOEN, KOMIPO, KOWEPO, KOSPO, EWP; ~53 GW combined) are being merged into a single "Korea Power Generation" entity by October 2027, with a dedicated Renewable Energy division (Sep 22). KPX opened its first ESS tender since the reform was announced — 1.18 GW/6.6 GWh, completion by February 2029 (Sep 25). And ESR, the Asia-Pacific real-asset manager, agreed to acquire Aquila Clean Energy APAC's 1.6 GW solar/BESS/wind platform, with Korea among its five markets (Sep 25) — foreign capital positioning itself ahead of the new system rather than waiting to see how it settles. Malaysia moved in a similar direction the same fortnight with its CRESS Acceleration Package (fixed grid-access charge, mandatory 10-year contracts, Sep 21) — a smaller, single-scheme version of the same instinct: replace open-ended compliance mechanisms with fixed, contractible terms.

Topic 3 · From three Philippines entries

Land control, not capital, is the swing variable in Philippine solar right now

Three entries this fortnight, same underlying constraint: ACEN voluntarily surrendered two early-stage solar contracts — 336 MW at Pagbilao and 120 MW in Zambales — citing land acquisition and site access problems, not financing (Sep 22). The same week, APECO and China's Huge Energy signed an MoU for a 1 GW solar+BESS+WTE complex in Casiguran, Aurora — made possible precisely because APECO, as the site's economic-zone authority, could hand over 90 hectares directly (Sep 25). And Basic Energy broke ground on its 43 MWac Cadiz 1 plant only after an 18-month path from Certificate of Authority (March 2025) through EPC signing (March 2026) to site works (September 2026) — even with land already secured (Sep 23). Read together: land control is now the variable separating projects that die (ACEN's two cancellations), projects that move fast (APECO's SEZ-backed MoU), and projects that grind through on a normal but lengthy timeline (Basic Energy) — more than capital availability, which none of the three entries cite as the constraint.
