Topic 1 · From five South Korea entries

South Korea rewrote its power market on a single day

30 September was unusually dense for a single day of policy movement: the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment held the public hearing on the detailed design of the auction-based CfD system replacing the RPS from 2027 (20-year fixed-price bilateral contracts with KEPCO, preferential pricing replacing the old REC multiplier); lifted Honam and Chungcheong grid connection restrictions that had blocked new solar for over two years — unlocking 2.6 GW without any new grid construction, using a flexible connection scheme with managed curtailment; and announced a target of 10 GW+ of new solar per year from 2027, against a first-half 2026 run rate of roughly 2 GW for the full half-year. Private platform companies that have built competing PPA-matching services also pushed back publicly against the Korean Energy Agency's proposed role as sole broker between RE generators and corporate buyers under the new system — a market-structure dispute that hasn't been resolved yet. The day before, Korea Zinc signed what looks like one of the first long-term fixed-price CPPAs to be structured ahead of the new system going live: 20 years, fixed price, 11 GWh/year from solar farms in three provinces, supply from December 2026. Corporate buyers aren't waiting for the transition to complete.

Topic 2 · From three Malaysia entries

Malaysia's CRESS has its first hyperscale anchor — and it's the biggest deal in the framework's history

Eight days after the CRESS Acceleration Package fixed the System Access Charge at MYR 0.14/kWh and mandated 10-year contracts (Sep 21), the G3nerasi Kinta consortium — SD Guthrie, Gamuda Energy and Gentari (PETRONAS) — signed a term sheet for a 680 MWac solar+BESS project in Perak, with an unnamed US-based multinational tech company as the offtaker on a 21-year Bilateral Energy Supply Contract, for estimated gross revenue of over RM10B across the contract term. That's the largest single CRESS commitment reported in the radar to date, and it likely wouldn't have been bankable at the previous MYR 0.20/0.40/kWh grid-access charge. Mah Sing and Kinergy Advancement (Oct 2) signed a non-binding term sheet the same week to evaluate captive power and CRESS frameworks for two data centre sites in Selangor and Johor — a different scale, but the same mechanism. The capacity backdrop helps explain why: Malaysia's operational data centre capacity is on track to nearly quadruple from 522 MW in Q1 2025 to 2,055 MW by end-2026, and pre-leased capacity hit 1.2 GW in H1 2026 — second in Asia-Pacific after Japan.

Topic 3 · From two Indonesia entries

Indonesia's 100 GWp programme is now in execution — two entries in two days show what that looks like in practice

The 100 GWp Solar Programme was formally launched in August; this week's two entries show what the transition from announcement to implementation actually involves. The Sep 30 entry reports 15 initial plants now in active development across six provinces — East Java, Central Java, West Java, Bali, Riau Islands and Bangka Belitung — with a projected Rp4.6T/year in state budget savings from reduced diesel consumption and Rp135T in total investment required. These aren't project proposals any more; they are confirmed government-led developments, and the government has said Danantara will step in if competitive IPP pricing cannot be achieved. Alongside that, the Sep 29 entry shows the RUPTL (Electricity Supply Business Plan) being formally revised to accommodate the 100 GWp target — with a plan to build 47,758 km of new transmission lines, target a national electrification rate above 99%, and push the energy resilience index to 7.13. The revision also explicitly flags that success could accelerate Indonesia's net-zero target by 10–15 years from 2060. And sitting alongside both: Sep 28 brings the Kolaka Nickel solar+BESS project (non-recourse financed by ICBC, StanChart and CITIC) — an example of industrial self-generation in the nickel sector, not part of the 100 GWp programme, but the same underlying trend of private RE adoption spreading beyond the utility sector.

Policy changes

Sep 30

South Korea lifts Honam and Chungcheong solar grid restrictions from 1 October — flexible connection scheme unlocks 2.6 GW; east coast restrictions to follow H1 2027

Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment lifted grid connection restrictions in Honam (Gwangju + North/South Jeolla) and Chungcheong regions effective 1 October, after new solar had been barred since June 2024 when grid capacity reached saturation. A new flexible connection scheme allows plants to connect even when distribution/transmission is technically full, subject to mandatory output curtailment at rated capacity — economically viable given solar's mid-10% utilisation rate. 2.6 GW of additional Honam solar is unlocked without new grid construction. East coast restrictions to be lifted H1 2027 when the first HVDC east coast–Singapyeong line completes; ~15.8 GW of offshore wind connection rights in Honam (~3 GW expected to proceed through 2030) remain separately subject to grid planning.
