Topic 1 · From three Malaysia entries (Oct 9, 10, 11)

Malaysia's Budget 2027 gives institutional backing to the RE push that the CRESS Acceleration Package started

The CRESS Acceleration Package fixed the grid access charge at MYR 0.14/kWh three weeks ago and the G3nerasi Kinta deal followed within days. Budget 2027, tabled 9 October, now turns those one-off policy moves into a fiscal commitment across three distinct lines: grid, incentives and direct investment. TNB's RM43B grid programme for 2025-2027 was already approved, but the budget explicitly designates RM15B of it for National Grid Strengthening — covering AI integration, BESS and the transmission capacity needed to absorb the data-centre load the CRESS deals are bringing in. Green investment tax allowances are restructured and extended through 31 December 2030: CRESS solar projects fall under GITA Project Tier 2 (60% allowance on qualifying capex, set off against 70% of statutory income). The RM1B KWAP Climate Fund is earmarked in full for RE development and decarbonisation. A RM1.5B centralised solar park for government buildings adds direct public procurement. And 150MW of the 2,500MW LSS6 programme is reserved for Bumiputera companies. Taken together, Budget 2027 is not just one-year spending — it fixes the RE investment framework through the end of the decade. Every CRESS developer and LSS6 bidder now has certainty on both the grid-access charge (MYR 0.14/kWh) and the tax treatment for the next four years.

Topic 2 · From three Vietnam entries (Oct 5, 8, 8)

Vietnam is restructuring the institution that holds its RE pipeline hostage — but the pipeline is still hostage while restructuring is underway

Vietnam's Electricity and Renewable Energy Authority announced this week that it is steering a multi-stage restructuring of EVN: EVNNPT (national transmission) to be separated from the group; state to divest stakes in EVN's generation companies; a new strategic national power corporation under consideration for multi-purpose hydro and nuclear assets; distribution and retail to be unbundled in a later stage. The stated goal is a competitive electricity market. That is the same structural direction South Korea and Malaysia have been moving, and the same problem every market in the radar is grappling with — how to get private RE capital into a grid that a state utility still controls. The complication is that EVN's existing relationship with the RE project pipeline is already broken. The same week, EPTC asked 172 wind and solar projects — representing billions in foreign investment — to renegotiate prices downward to the transitional tariff ceilings from Decision 21/2023. Developers dispute the legal basis for retroactive application; the dispute has been running since mid-2025. The projects are generating electricity; the tariff question determines whether the equity is worth anything. Restructuring EVN makes sense as a long-term institution-building move. But the 172 projects sitting in a tariff limbo do not benefit from a restructuring that will take years to implement — and S&P's October 7 gas weekly (in the S&P section below) shows the immediate consequence: Vietnam's gas-fired generation has surged to ~3 aGW, the strongest year-over-year increase in Southeast Asia, because RE is not filling the gap fast enough.

Topic 3 · From three South Korea entries (Oct 7, 11, 11)

South Korea announced its biggest energy investment commitment in history — the same week a fourth major foreign investor explained why they left

The Korea Green Transformation (K-GX) plan — KRW 1,000 trillion ($747B) through 2035, including KRW 200T in fiscal spending — sets the most concrete numerical target to date for Korea's 100 GW RE goal, adds a 2040 coal phase-out roadmap, and dedicates sector-specific decarbonisation blueprints to steel, petrochemicals, refining, cement and semiconductors. In the same week, Iberdrola explained why it left after signing a 2021 MOU with GS Energy: PPA terms and REC calculation methods can change with each new administration, making 20-year investment decisions impossible. That is not a new complaint — it is exactly what Equinor said before halting its Ulsan and Chuja Island projects, what Corio said before closing its Korean subsidiary in March 2026, and what Shell said before its offshore wind exit. The domestic capital that has stepped in (SK/KKR, Brookfield's KRW 30T commitment, and this week's first wind-farm repowering by a Korean financial investor, Truston) is a real alternative. But the K-GX plan needs a scale of capital that domestic sources alone have not been able to mobilise for offshore wind. The plan's KRW 200T in fiscal funding is substantial; how much of it flows through structures that foreign institutional capital can access will determine whether the 100 GW target produces a domestic industry or a delayed one.

Budget 2027 GITA Restructured and Extended to 31 December 2030 — CRESS Solar Projects Qualify Under GITA Project Tier 2 (60% Allowance, 70% Income Set-Off); GITA Asset Tier 1 Applies Only to Self-Consuming Off-takers

Budget 2027 restructures GITA for assets verified 1 January 2027–31 December 2030. GITA Project (for income-generating RE projects including CRESS): Tier 1 (green hydrogen, OTEC): 100% allowance; Tier 2 (RE systems, EV charging): 60% allowance, 70% statutory income set-off — this is the category covering CRESS solar projects. GITA Asset (own-consumption only) does not apply to CRESS or third-party supply; it covers assets used solely by the off-taking company for its own operations. The distinction matters for developer tax planning: CRESS developers use GITA Project Tier 2; their corporate off-takers may separately claim GITA Asset Tier 1 (100% allowance, 100% or 70% set-off) on their own-consumption RE assets.

Thailand Committee Moves to Renegotiate or Cancel Unsigned Legacy Renewable PPAs — Solar Target Rate 2.1579 THB/kWh, Wind 2.9015 THB/kWh; Only 2–3% of ~400–500 Contracts Have Agreed So Far

A government committee chaired by Deputy Prime Minister Pakorn Nilprapunt resolved at its third meeting to renegotiate or cancel unsigned power purchase agreements with private renewable producers. Where signing is unavoidable, purchase rates will be reset to NEPC-approved rates: 2.1579 baht/unit for solar; 2.9015 baht/unit for wind; or a rate more favourable to consumers. The committee found only ~2–3% of the approximately 400–500 contracts requiring revision had agreed to amend or cancel. The government's position: state utilities should buy solar at ~2.15 baht/unit and wind at ~2.90 baht/unit — materially below many legacy contract rates set when fuel-price adders ('Adder') were standard. The dispute affects the broader 4,108 MW of non-firm SPP and VSPP non-firm contracts that the committee has been reviewing since July 2026.

EVN's EPTC Asks 172 Renewable Energy Projects to Negotiate Lower Tariffs — Covers Periods of Operation Without Written Construction Acceptance Approval; Developers Dispute Retroactive Application

EVN's Electricity Power Trading Company asked 172 grid-connected wind and solar projects to negotiate lower prices for periods when they were operating commercially but lacked written construction acceptance inspection approval. Negotiated prices should not exceed Decision 21/2023 transitional tariff ceilings: VND 1,184.9–1,508.27/kWh (solar); VND 1,587.12–1,815.95/kWh (wind), depending on project type. Developers dispute the retroactive application, arguing that regulations in force when some projects began commercial operations did not require written approval as a COD condition, and that Circular 10/2023 introduced new requirements that cannot be applied retroactively to prior periods. The dispute involves some of the largest foreign-invested wind and solar portfolios in the country and has been ongoing since mid-2025.

South Korea's current wind and solar capacity is saving an estimated $4.7B annually in fossil fuel import costs.
