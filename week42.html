Topic 1 · From three Malaysia entries (Oct 13, 14, 16)

Malaysia reversed the CRESS SAC cut within three weeks — grid load shift anxiety, not project economics, drove the reversal

The CRESS Acceleration Package on 21 September set the System Access Charge at MYR 0.14/kWh for firm solar+BESS. Three weeks later, on 14 October, the Energy Commission and PETRA briefed approximately 100 industry participants on an additional 4 sen/kWh surcharge — effective immediately and applicable until end-2027, bringing the SAC to 18 sen/kWh. Only 2 sen below the pre-September level of 20 sen. The stated reason was concern that a 14 sen SAC was too attractive — it would pull existing industrial customers off the national grid, reducing the load and revenue base for TNB's existing generation fleet, and potentially raising per-unit tariffs for remaining consumers under Regulatory Period 5 (2028–2030). The Deputy Minister's statement from October 13 reveals the context: of 62 data centres that had signed electricity supply agreements, 45 had begun operations — but combined actual consumption was only 1.37 GW against a contracted maximum load of 6 GW. The actual demand from data centres is running far below contracted capacity. Against that background, allowing large industrials to switch to CRESS at 14 sen — potentially reducing grid load further — was seen as a tariff design risk the government wasn't willing to accept. The reversal is significant because it happened before a single CRESS project beyond the HLGY/Bridge DC project in Kedah had broken ground at the 14 sen rate. Samaiden's CRESS EPCC pipeline, priced on 14 sen bankability assumptions, now faces a changed cost structure. The Budget 2027 green incentives (GITA, KWAP fund, government solar park) remain intact, but the grid access charge — the most important CRESS enabler — is now back in limbo pending RP5 methodology in 2028.

Topic 2 · From two Indonesia entries (Oct 12, 14)

Prabowo replaced PLN's CEO hours after summoning him to the palace — and the new CEO is a renewables and project execution specialist

The sequence matters: on 14 October, President Prabowo summoned Suroso Isnandar — PLN's Director of Project Management and New and Renewable Energy — to the State Palace alongside Danantara CEO Rosan Roeslani and ESDM Minister Bahlil Lahadalia. Prabowo issued instructions to overhaul PLN's business model, increase efficiency and productivity, reduce the subsidy burden, and accelerate 100 GWp of solar panel deployment. Hours later, PLN held a virtual General Meeting of Shareholders and confirmed Suroso as the new President Director. The outgoing CEO, Darmawan Prasodjo, had served since December 2021 and was associated with the structural narrative around Indonesia's energy transition. The appointment of his successor — a career PLN insider whose most recent role was specifically running the renewable energy and project management division — signals a switch from structural ambition to execution priority. The Batam blackout earlier in the week, and prior large outages in Sumatra and Kalimantan through 2026, provided the operational pressure. This week's other Indonesia entry adds nuance: PLN's planned T-REC launch in 2028 — hourly supply-demand matching for data centres and RE100 corporates — shows the commercial side of the utility is actively building toward the market that the 100 GWp programme is meant to supply. The combined picture is of a utility under maximum pressure to deliver megawatts, while simultaneously trying to build the commercial infrastructure to monetise them.

Topic 3 · From three Philippines entries (Oct 7 manual add, Oct 16, Oct 19)

Vena Energy is running the most aggressive capital deployment sequence in the Philippines radar this year

Three entries in ten days tell a tight story. July 30: Vena reaches financial close on the 500 MWp Ixus Bugallon at $310M from 8 international banks. October 7 (the missed entry, now added): the ERC extends the GEA-All charge suspension through December 2026, citing the PhP556.9M fund balance — regulatory continuity for projects like Ixus already in the programme. October 16: Norfund (Norwegian state development finance institution) and Impact Fund Denmark acquire a combined 49% equity stake in Ixus Bugallon — development finance coming in behind the commercial debt. October 19: Vena announces an additional P245 billion of investment to add 2.6 GWp to its existing ~0.9 GWp Philippine portfolio over two years. The sequence — financial close, development finance equity, then a large expansion announcement — is the fastest such sequence in the Philippine radar for any single developer. Vena's existing Philippine capacity (887 MW operational, ~1.26 GW under construction) already makes it one of the country's largest RE operators; the additional 2.6 GWp would more than double the current and construction-stage combined. For context: the entire GEA-4 was 10.2 GW across 123 projects; one developer announcing 2.6 GWp in a single week is notable concentration of capital at one player.

Oct 14

CRESS System Access Charge Revised Up to 18 Sen/kWh — 4 Sen Temporary Surcharge Added to September's 14 Sen Rate; Concerns Over Consumer Load Shift Away From National Grid Drove Reversal

The government has effectively raised the CRESS System Access Charge to 18 sen/kWh — a partial reversal of the September 2026 CRESS Acceleration Package that had cut the SAC from 20 to 14 sen/kWh. An Energy Commission and PETRA briefing attended by approximately 100 participants introduced an additional 4 sen/kWh applicable until end-2027, "subject to Regulatory Period review" (every three years). The reversal was driven by concern that a 14 sen SAC would attract too many existing industrial customers to switch to CRESS, reducing national grid utilisation and potentially increasing per-unit base tariffs for remaining consumers in RP5 (2028–2030). Context: only one CRESS project is currently under advanced construction — HLGY Energy/Bridge Data Centres in Kedah. The previously announced 14 sen rate would have made CRESS competitive with the grid tariff for many industrial users. The government has confirmed it will develop a more transparent, cost-reflective SAC methodology from RP5 onwards. This is a significant mid-course correction that changes the bankability assumptions for CRESS pipeline deals priced at 14 sen.

Deputy Energy Transition and Water Transformation Minister Datuk Seri Abdul Rahman Mohamad proposed requiring data centre operators to develop their own power generation facilities (solar, BESS) and their own water supply for cooling. As at 1 September 2026, renewables accounted for nearly 32% of Malaysia's installed capacity (~14,000 MW). Targets: 35% RE installed capacity by 2030, 70% by 2050; an additional 10,000–11,000 MW of RE expected online by 2030.

PLN Plans to Launch Time-Based Renewable Energy Certificates (T-REC) in 2028 — Hourly Supply-Demand Matching, Source-Identified Green Power; Response to Data Centre and RE100 Corporate Demand

PLN's VP for Commercial Product Planning and Strategy Bobby Cristya Surya announced at the Indonesia International Sustainability Forum 2026 (Jakarta, 6 October) that PLN plans to launch Time-based Renewable Energy Certificates in 2028. T-REC will build on PLN's existing monthly REC service, adding hourly granularity: customers can identify which power plant is supplying their electricity and assess the 'matching rate' between their hourly consumption and renewable energy generation (example cited: 90%). Currently in the pilot stage with several partners. Drivers: corporate sustainability reporting requirements, RE100 supply chain mandates, and growing data centre demand — with seven to eight data centres expected to enter Indonesia, requiring green electricity services. A 2028 launch would align with PLN's broader market-building under the new CEO's execution mandate.

Oct 12

South Korea Publishes Draft Rules for New RE Auction Contract Market — Monthly CfD Payments from KEPCO, Separate 500 kW Track, REC Spot Market Closes End-2029, First Tender Q1 2027

The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment published draft implementing rules for the auction-based contract market replacing the RPS from 1 January 2027. Key provisions: KEPCO signs long-term fixed-price contracts with winning projects on behalf of all obligated entities, settling the difference between contract price and wholesale price monthly (CfD structure). Two to four tenders per year by energy source, with auction volumes rising under 5-year roadmaps. A public-private cost evaluation committee sets annual ceiling prices reflecting generation costs plus a reasonable return. Scoring: price plus non-price factors (community acceptance, domestic industry contribution, grid impact). Preferential prices replace REC multipliers for floating solar, agrivoltaic, rooftop solar, projects in spare-grid areas, community-owned projects, and supply-chain/energy-security contributions. Separate market track for projects of 500 kW or less. Large public generators (≥500 MW) must participate in competitive tenders under a 'collective compliance' system. The REC spot market closes end-2029; existing RPS contracts continue at agreed terms through their remaining life.

Equinor Exits South Korean Offshore Wind Market After 12 Years — All Ulsan and Chuja Island Projects Halted; Company Rules Out Future Opportunities

Equinor has formally exited South Korea's offshore wind market after approximately 12 years of attempted development, halting all its offshore wind projects — including large-scale floating and fixed-bottom developments off Ulsan and Jeju's Chuja Island — and stating it will not pursue future opportunities in South Korea. The exit follows Shell (2024), Corio Generation (March 2026) and Iberdrola (2026). South Korea's domestic capital — through SK/KKR, Brookfield (KRW 30T commitment), and this month's Truston wind repowering — continues to fill the gap left by foreign developers pulling back.
