Topic 1 · From Malaysia (Nov 11, Nov 14)

Malaysia is moving data centres onto their own wholesale market while it reviews how fuel costs reach other consumers

On 11 November the Energy Commission's director of market regulation and planning, Rumaizi Abdul Halim, said Malaysia intends to launch a separate wholesale electricity market for large consumers, including data centres. The existing single-buyer market, which serves households, factories and commercial premises, stays as it is, the two markets will run under one operator, and how they interact is to be discussed later. Three days later PETRA and the Energy Commission said they are studying a tiered Automatic Fuel Adjustment (AFA), planned from 2027, which could vary by consumer category or usage level. The AFA is the monthly part of a Peninsular Malaysia bill that passes through gas and coal generation costs, and PETRA framed the review as a cost-of-living measure. The tiers, thresholds and rates are not published, and the 800 kWh domestic bill protection ends on 31 December. These sit on top of measures already in the radar: the Ultra-High Voltage tariff for data centres since July 2025, an increased Connected Load Charge, a 30% minimum renewable requirement and the 4 sen/kWh CRESS surcharge through end-2027. The 20 October ministerial reply framed those as a cost causation principle, meant to avoid passing data centre costs to other consumers, and the new market continues that direction. For offtakers, the open points are how the new market prices power and how it relates to CRESS.

Topic 2 · From Vietnam (Nov 13)

Vietnam sets its first standalone BESS price ceiling, VND 3,209.98/kWh, with prices set on lifecycle cost and a 12% IRR cap

Decision 2987/QD-BCT, effective 11 November, is Vietnam's first generation-service price framework for standalone battery storage. The ceiling is VND 3,209.98 per kWh (about US$0.12) excluding VAT, and it applies to storage projects of 10 MW or more connected to the national grid at 110 kV or above. Actual prices are set under Circular 62/2025/TT-BCT on a full lifecycle cost basis with an internal rate of return cap of 12%. It follows the 5 November Decision 2946, which set solar-plus-storage ceilings, so co-located and standalone storage now each have an official reference, although the two are priced on different bases and should not be compared directly per kWh. The entry notes that PDP8 targets 10,000 to 16,300 MW of storage by 2030 and that independent projects now have a reference for financing and offtake negotiations. Two limits are visible in the radar. The 10 MW and 110 kV thresholds would exclude smaller distribution-connected systems such as the 10 MW / 10 MWh Thanh Hoa battery energised at 22 kV in Week 45. And the entry does not say whether the 12% cap is a project or an equity return. For reference, an earlier entry cites Ember's estimate of Vietnam utility solar IRR at 6.1% (7.3% with BESS), which is a different asset type and not a like-for-like comparison.

Topic 3 · From the Philippines island electrification entry (Nov 13)

The Philippines joins Vietnam and Indonesia on replacing island diesel with solar and BESS, and the DOE plans to fold it into the GEA

The partnership was launched on 10 November at the COP31 Philippine Hub in Antalya, Turkiye, to replace diesel generators in remote and island communities with solar PV and battery storage, aiming to improve electricity access, cut dependence on imported fossil fuels and lower generation costs. DOE Undersecretary Felix William Fuentebella said the government plans to integrate island electrification into its Green Energy Auction programme, and that specific auction rounds, timelines and capacity allocations are not final. The scale is set by the off-grid fleet: as of December 2025, about 94% of the Philippines' off-grid power plants (421 of 447) relied heavily on oil. On Cobrador Island in Romblon, a 30 kW solar-plus-battery hybrid cut generation costs by nearly 50%, from $0.47 to $0.24 per kWh, and raised availability from eight hours a day to 24 hours. Indonesia's programme is further along: its 100 GW solar drive prioritises an initial 13 GW of solar plus BESS to replace about 5,200 diesel generators in remote and island regions, where an August entry cites diesel at US$0.29 to 0.65 per kWh against US$0.08 to 0.20 for solar plus BESS. For Philippine developers, the open question is whether island projects arrive as a separate GEA category and on what timeline.

PETRA and Energy Commission Study Tiered Automatic Fuel Adjustment (AFA) Planned From 2027; 800 kWh Domestic Bill Protection Ends 31 December

Malaysia's Ministry of Energy Transition and Water Transformation (PETRA) said in an X thread posted on Saturday that it and the Energy Commission are examining improvements to the existing Automatic Fuel Adjustment (AFA) mechanism, including a tiered AFA planned to begin in 2027. The post cited a Dewan Rakyat session on 19 October 2026. The AFA is the monthly component of a Peninsular Malaysia electricity bill that passes through the cost of generating power, adding a surcharge when gas and coal costs rise and a rebate when they fall. A tiered AFA could vary by consumer category or usage level; the ministry has not published the tiers, thresholds or rates. PETRA framed it as a cost-of-living measure so that tariff decisions are balanced and supply stays affordable, secure and sustainable. The article cites Bernama reporting that Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof told Parliament on 27 October that higher bills for some domestic users were partly driven by a global fuel price surge. The 800 kWh domestic bill protection ends on 31 December.

Nov 3

South Korea Opens 2026 Hydrogen Power Bidding Market: 500 GWh a Year Clean Hydrogen Segment, 15-Year Contracts, Non-Price Weighting Raised From 40% to 50%, Coal-Ammonia Co-Firing Excluded

South Korea's Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment opened its 2026 hydrogen power generation bidding market. The clean hydrogen segment is set at 500 GWh a year with a 15-year contract period, and projects must begin generating by 2030 after a three-year preparation period. The selection process was revised: a first-stage assessment now uses non-price criteria only, and shortlisted projects are then scored on price and non-price criteria with the non-price weight raised from 40% to 50%. Non-price factors include clean hydrogen utilisation, fuel supply security, greenhouse gas reductions and contributions to industry and the economy. Coal-ammonia co-firing projects are excluded from the bidding market from this year. Selected bidders are expected to be announced around June 2027, and a grace period of up to three years is allowed for project delays before penalties apply.

SunCable Largely Finalises Subsea Cable Route From Australia Through Indonesian Waters; Financial Close Now Around 2030, Supply to Singapore Around 2035; EMA Calls 6 GW Import Target Aspirational

SunCable chief operating officer Mitesh Patel said the company has largely finalised the subsea cable route from Australia to Singapore waters, with only the final stretch within Singapore waters still to be determined. SunCable ran its first Indonesian survey in 2022 and further studies early this year. The Australia-Asia PowerLink would transmit 1.75 GW from a Powell Creek solar farm via Darwin through a subsea cable of about 4,300 km, of which around 3,700 km is in Indonesian waters; Patel said the cable will only pass through Indonesian waters and will not connect to Indonesia's grid. The project received conditional approval from the Singapore government in 2024, and SunCable now expects to supply Singapore around 2035. Patel expects financial close around 2030, about three years later than the timeline disclosed in 2024, saying the revision reflects development progress and that subsea cable studies need more time. The headline notes that the EMA calls the 6 GW import target aspirational.
