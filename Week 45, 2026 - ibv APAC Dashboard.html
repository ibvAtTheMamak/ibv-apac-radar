Topic 1 · From Malaysia (Nov 9, Nov 6)

Malaysia's CRESS pipeline kept adding data centre offtake after the October surcharge, with TNB on both the supply and offtake side of the Bahau deal

Two CRESS entries this week add data centre offtake to the pipeline after the 14 October revision of the system access charge to 18 sen/kWh, which applies until end-2027. TNB Renewables and IOI Corporation agreed to develop a 500 MWac solar project with 250 MW / 1,000 MWh of storage on about 1,300 acres of IOI land in Bahau, Negeri Sembilan. TNB Renewables holds 70% of the project company, IOI's Luminous Aspect 25% and CSP 500MW 5%, and DayOne Data Centres is expected to procure up to 500 MWac under a 21-year offtake agreement with TNB. Separately, Northern Solar secured two CRESS contracts totalling 189 MWac of solar with BESS for two data centres: 100 MWac for a data centre developed by a Malaysian property investment and data centre infrastructure company (annual need 262.8 million kWh) and 89 MWac awarded by a Malaysian subsidiary of a Singapore-based data centre operator (233.89 million kWh a year), together nearly 500 million kWh a year. Neither entry states a tariff or SAC assumption, so they show contracting continuing after the surcharge rather than the terms. The Bahau storage sizing works out to 50% of export capacity and four hours (250 MW against 500 MWac, 1,000 MWh over 250 MW), the firm-supply BESS pairing cited in the 21 September CRESS entry; those ratios are derived from the two figures. Alongside the 29 September G3nerasi Kinta term sheet, the three deals put roughly 1.4 GW of CRESS solar in front of data centre buyers over six weeks, at different stages (sum derived from the entries).

Topic 2 · From Vietnam (Nov 9, Nov 4, Nov 7)

Vietnam's 2026 solar price ceilings are up 7% to 17% on 2025, with the biggest rises in the Central region, which includes Dak Lak

Decision 2946/QD-BCT, effective 5 November, sets 2026 maximum solar generation prices by region and type under Circular 09/2025. Against the 2025 ceilings in the comparison table, every solar category rises: by 13.0% to 15.4% in the North, 14.5% to 16.9% in the Central region and 6.9% to 9.1% in the South. For solar paired with BESS the rise runs from 6.9% (South, floating) to 16.9% (Central, ground-mounted). The highest 2026 ceiling is VND 2,120.31/kWh for floating solar with BESS in the North and the lowest is VND 1,104.32/kWh for ground-mounted without BESS in the South. The decision also redraws the zoning: three regions covering 34 provinces and cities, with 18 in the North, 8 in the Central region (Quang Tri, Hue, Quang Ngai, Da Nang, Gia Lai, Dak Lak, Khanh Hoa and Lam Dong) and 8 in the South. A separate third-party project, the 452 MW Ea Sup Thuong floating solar project with BESS in Dak Lak (not ib vogt's own Dak Lak project), was approved on 31 October with US$355 million of investment and completion by end-2028. It falls in the Central floating-with-BESS category, where the ceiling is VND 1,703.21/kWh, up 14.5% from VND 1,487.18. That is the largest rise for floating with BESS across the three regions. The Dak Lak entry gives no tariff for the project, and these are maximum prices rather than awarded tariffs. The table also shows wind ceilings higher, from 4.8% (Central onshore) to 11.7% (North onshore); Gia Lai, in the Central zone for solar, broke ground on the 180 MW Van Canh 2 wind farm this week (VND 7,700 billion, operation in 2028).

Topic 3 · From the Philippines NGCP entries (Nov 1, Nov 1)

NGCP is pressing ahead with 850 MW of Project Airbag batteries while asking the DOE to withdraw the circulars that would let others build transmission

The imminent piece is NGCP's Project Airbag. At a media chat on 29 October, Henry Sy Jr., chairman of NGCP's parent Synergy Grid & Development Philippines, pitched a nationwide chain of battery storage, and NGCP says 850 MW is already under way across six sites (Laoag, Gamu, Santiago, Calatrava, Dumanjug and Ubay), costed at about P59.5 billion, with another 1,000 MW across 12 sites under assessment. The wider pitch of 10,000 MW within five years, at about P700 billion (about $11 billion), is the longer-range ambition, and the article notes it is not an approved budget, MWh is not reported and ownership is unresolved. Sy framed batteries as a bridge to ease high electricity costs and prepare for a possible El Nino in 2027, not a replacement for dependable generation. The second entry matters as much for developers. In a statement on 1 November, NGCP asked the DOE to withdraw two circulars that let TransCo and generation companies finance or build transmission, arguing they do not fix the regulatory, permitting and right-of-way delays, that projects outside the Transmission Development Plan would complicate coordination of construction schedules, design and grid integration, and that overlapping planning and review would blur accountability. It cited a February 2025 arbitral award on its exclusive responsibility for the plan. Read together, NGCP is expanding its own role in storage while resisting others building the wires, and the circulars leave that question with the DOE. Earlier radar entries show where the strain sits: the Visayas grid logged 134 yellow and red alerts through 14 September, and the DOE endorsed 137 projects (17.8 GW) for grid impact study, with batteries the largest group at 85 projects.

NGCP Asks DOE to Withdraw Two Circulars Allowing Gencos and TransCo to Finance or Build Transmission, Saying They Won't Fix Permitting and Right-of-Way Delays

NGCP is seeking withdrawal of two DOE circulars that allow other entities to finance or build transmission facilities, one covering the state-owned National Transmission Corp. (TransCo) and the other covering power generation companies. In a Sunday statement it said it submitted a consolidated position paper to Energy Secretary Sharon Garin. NGCP argues the circulars will not resolve the regulatory, permitting and right-of-way constraints that delay grid projects. It objects to provisions allowing projects outside the Transmission Development Plan (TDP), saying separately planned facilities could complicate coordination of construction schedules, design and grid integration, and that overlapping planning and review processes could complicate accountability and cause further delays. It cited a 19 February 2025 arbitral award which it said affirmed its exclusive responsibility to prepare the TDP during the concession period.

Nov 9

Indonesia Targets Reservoirs and Lakes for Floating Solar to Meet 100 GW Solar Target Over Three Years; ESDM Estimates Up to 90 GW Across 250 Water Bodies, Jatigede and Jatiluhur Next for Auctions

Facing land constraints on its most densely populated islands, Indonesia is turning to lakes and reservoirs to meet a new target of installing 100 GW of solar capacity over the next three years. Total national generation capacity is approximately 88 GW, with solar at 1.5 GW. The Energy and Mineral Resources Ministry estimates up to 90 GW of floating solar potential across 250 inland water bodies. The Cirata reservoir in West Java, built by PLN and Masdar and operating since 2023 with 340,000 floating panels for about 50,000 homes, is cited as the largest floating solar plant in Southeast Asia and the model. Planners are targeting existing hydro dams to reduce infrastructure costs, since they already have substations and transmission lines, and the hybrid model lets operators rely on solar in daytime while conserving reservoir water for hydro at night. Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said the programme could save up to Rp73.9 trillion (US$4.1 billion) a year in energy subsidies.

Dak Lak Approves $355M, 452 MW Floating Solar Project at Ea Sup Thuong Reservoir with BESS, Completion by End-2028

South Korea's Installed Renewable Capacity Overtakes Coal for the First Time: 41,040 MW vs 40,757 MW at 21 October 2026, 25.4% of Total, Second Behind LNG

Per the power exchange KPX, South Korea's installed renewable capacity exceeded coal for the first time. On the 21 October 2026 reference date, renewables stood at 41,040 MW against 40,757 MW for coal, a lead of 283 MW, equal to 25.4 percent of total installed generation capacity, placing renewables second behind LNG at 46,788 MW (a gap of about 5,748 MW). The figures are installed capacity, not electricity generated. From end 2020 to end 2025 renewable capacity grew by about 3,504 MW a year on average, against about 807 MW a year for LNG. KPX assumes that at the current pace renewables could surpass LNG capacity within two to three years and become the largest source by installed capacity. The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment targets 100 GW of renewables by 2030 and at least 30 percent of generation from renewables by 2035; asked about bringing the 100 GW target forward, the ministry said it is still being discussed.
