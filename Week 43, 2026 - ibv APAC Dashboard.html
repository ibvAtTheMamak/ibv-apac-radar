Topic 1 · From Malaysia (Oct 20) and Indonesia (Oct 19)

Malaysia and Indonesia are both trying to steer data centre load, one through approvals and price signals, the other through siting

Malaysia's 20 October ministerial reply and Indonesia's 19 October data centre hub announcement describe two different ways of managing data centre load. Malaysia is working through approvals and price signals. The reply puts approved data centre electricity supply capacity at 12.3 GW as of August, against a projected sector maximum demand of 8.39 GW by 2030, and the tools it cites are aimed at over-subscription: an Ultra-High Voltage tariff in force since July 2025, an increased Connected Load Charge described as a penalty to stop operators applying for more capacity than they need, and a requirement that data centres source at least 30% renewable energy, which can be met through CRESS or Solar for Self-Consumption. The gap between approved and actually used capacity was already visible in the 13 October entry, where contracted maximum load was 6 GW against about 1.37 GW of actual consumption. Indonesia's announcement is at an earlier stage. Minister Bahlil said the government is pushing a new hub across Batang, Karimun and Bintan to draw investment away from Jakarta, West Java and Bali toward areas with water supply and lower population density, with renewable energy, including about 100 GWp of planned solar, meant to supply it. No project list, investment figure or timeline was given. Malaysia is tightening conditions on load it has already approved; Indonesia is still designing where load should go.

Topic 2 · From three South Korea entries (Oct 20, 20, 25)

South Korea is filling in the market around the RPS replacement before it starts on 1 January

Three entries show Korea adding pieces around the auction system that replaces the RPS on 1 January. On the demand side, the ministry plans renewable energy target management for public institutions, including local public enterprises, split between capacity installed directly and idle land provided to generators, with Korea Expressway Corporation and Korail expected to fall under the land-based targets. A survey of idle public land comes first, and the target system is planned ahead of competitive bidding in the first half after the RPS ends. On the commercial side, a third mega-zone bill, which Rep. Jeong Jin-wook is close to filing, would extend the PPA system, today limited to renewable generators, to sources such as LNG cogeneration, and give zone companies up to a 50% electricity bill exemption and a waiver of Electric Power Industry Basis Fund levies. On permitting, a national MCEE guideline now treats a sealed outdoor BESS that people cannot enter as a structure rather than a building, which the entry says can cut up to three months from approval. The LNG element has a counterpart elsewhere in the radar: on 7 September, Ho Chi Minh City's trade department drafted a resolution to add LNG-fired plants as eligible DPPA generators. In both places the proposal is still a draft. The stated reason in Korea is offsetting renewable intermittency, and in Ho Chi Minh City it is energy security.

Topic 3 · From the Philippines Citicore financing entry (Oct 23)

Citicore's P35B Batangas financing puts a three-bank syndicate behind 900 MWp of GEA-4 solar and 120 MWh of storage

Citicore Renewable Energy Corporation's P35.014 billion syndicated loan from BPI, LANDBANK and DBP is its largest to date and finances five Batangas projects: two 300 MWp solar plants and three 100 MWp plants, each paired with a 40 MWh BESS. All offtake has already been awarded under GEA-4, so the lenders are financing contracted revenue. Set against other Philippine solar and storage financings logged in the radar over the last two months, the deal is a step up in scale. PNB co-financed PHP 6.215 billion for Levanta's 166 MWp Iloilo solar+BESS in August, RCBC provided PHP 2.3 billion for ACEN and Yanara's 75 MW Sual project, and Alternergy's debut green notes raised PHP 2 billion to fund GEA-4 pre-development. The five projects are expected to generate about 1,300 GWh a year.

Malaysia Has Approved 12.3 GW of Electricity Supply Capacity for Data Centres as of August; Sector Maximum Demand Projected at 8.39 GW by 2030

In a written parliamentary reply, Energy Transition and Water Transformation Minister Fadillah Yusof said data centre projects had secured approval for 12.3 GW of electricity supply capacity as of August, with maximum demand from the sector projected to reach 8.39 GW by 2030. The government will expand generation capacity and strengthen grid infrastructure in phases. To avoid passing data centre costs to other consumers under a cost causation principle, the reply cites the Ultra-High Voltage tariff, in force since 1 July 2025 for new and existing data centres supplied above 50 kV, and an increased Connected Load Charge, a penalty mechanism to stop data centres applying for capacity above their actual needs. Data centres must use at least 30% renewable energy based on their total demand capacity, which can be met through CRESS and the Solar for Self-Consumption initiative. The industry is encouraged to develop self-generation facilities.

South Korea Plans Renewable Energy Target System for Public Institutions Including Idle Land Provision, to Start Alongside RPS Abolition on 1 January

The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment is pushing to introduce a renewable energy target management system for public institutions, including local public enterprises, in line with the abolition of the RPS on 1 January next year. Targets will be split into the capacity of RE facilities an institution installs directly, and the scale of idle land it provides to RE generators. Land-based targets are intended for public institutions that are not power generation enterprises; Korea Expressway Corporation and Korail are expected to be prime examples. The ministry plans a comprehensive survey of idle land held by public institutions and local public enterprises. A ministry official said competitive bidding will be held in the first half after the RPS is abolished, and the target system is planned before then. The government targets more than 100 GW of renewable capacity by 2030. This year it added K-RE100 participation to public institution management evaluation criteria.

Third Korean Mega-Zone Bill Would Extend PPAs to Non-Renewables Such as LNG Cogeneration and Cut Zone Electricity Bills by Up to 50%

Democratic Party Rep. Jeong Jin-wook is close to filing a mega-zone bill, as early as the following week. The draft would extend the PPA system, currently available only to renewable generators under existing law, to sources such as LNG cogeneration, to offset renewable intermittency; semiconductor production, which needs large amounts of electricity and high-temperature steam at the same time, could see efficiency gains from direct supply from cogeneration plants. Companies in the zones would receive an exemption cutting electricity bills by up to 50% and a waiver of levies for the Electric Power Industry Basis Fund. Military service exemption incentives for employees of designated firms are also included. This is the third mega-zone bill: the Democratic Party's National Policy Committee plans a version with an exemption from the 52-hour workweek cap, and Rep. Choi Ki-sang plans a version without it.

South Korea MCEE National Guideline Classes Sealed Outdoor BESS as a Structure, Cutting Permitting by Up to 3 Months

The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment (MCEE) issued a national guideline that could cut up to three months from permitting of an outdoor BESS. Previously each local government used its own policy, so the same unit could be classed as a building in one district and a structure in the next. A building has required a permit process of about two to four months; a structure about half a month to one and a half months. The guideline adds human access, structure and use as tests, so a sealed enclosure that cannot be entered will be classed as a structure. Land permits and fire safety rules still apply to each site as before.
